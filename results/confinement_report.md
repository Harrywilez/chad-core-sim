# Actual magnetic confinement search in Chad Core Lab

The teams found a stronger simulated confinement design in the real Lab model. On three fresh panels totaling **1,440 common particles per design**, the build-and-explore team's locked primary retained particles for an average **50.84 wall transits**, versus **18.06** for the baseline and **31.96** for the direct-search primary. Its baseline ratio is **2.82×**. Both nominees improved on baseline in every panel, and the construction team's nominee exceeded the direct nominee in every panel.

This is a positive result for these particular search trajectories. It supports further tests of the environment-building idea; one paired run cannot establish that the strategy generally works better. These are vacuum test-particle measurements, not demonstrated plasma confinement or an engineering-ready reactor.

## The actual selected field

The leading assembly is an opposed pair of closed toroidal windings generated by the existing `hopfmirror` family: eta 0.90, circuits parameter 32, two revolutions, delta_eta 0.03, woven mode, sense −1. In this parameter regime the resulting conductor layout is nested. The two physical circuits carry opposite currents with magnitude 149,422.4 A. Total active conductor length is 213.61 m; declared conductor radius is 4.48 mm and validated clearance is 11.33 mm. A conductor-layout label does not establish nested magnetic flux surfaces.

The exact design is saved in Chad Core Lab at `designs/confinement-study/construction-primary.json` and in this study at `locked/construction-primary.json`. The direct primary, alternates, and parent controls are saved beside it. Names, parameters and hashes were locked before held-out feedback.

## Fresh-particle results

| Build-and-explore primary | 50.507 | 47.861 | 54.152 | 50.840 | 30.14% |

All means are capped at 120 wall transits, with retained particles contributing 120. Every design sees the same explicit initial positions and velocities within each panel, 15 keV deuterons, physical current cap, wire radius, vessel, field grid, and integration settings. Initial conductor overlaps count as immediate losses. No candidate-specific launch filtering, adaptive horizon, fitted tail or buildability multiplier enters this endpoint.

The construction-primary baseline improvement is **32.785 transits**; its conditional paired-marker 95% interval is [30.194, 35.597]. Its improvement over the direct primary is **18.881**, with interval [16.496, 21.143]. These intervals resample markers within the three fixed panels. They do not quantify variation between AI teams, numerical-model error, or a population strategy effect.

Both locked alternates also completed all three panels. Their means were 21.637 for direct and 45.956 for construction. They remain exploratory alternatives and did not replace the preselected primaries after evaluation.

The finer grid uses 33 points per axis instead of 25. The smaller time step halves the original step. The separate collision diagnostic checks the finite wire segments, including initial overlaps, instead of only sampled strand points. Its comparison uses the same first 160 particles from held-out panel 1 for all three designs.

For that identical prefix, original point-collision means were 20.481, 33.803 and 49.366; the segment correction lowered them to 18.996, 31.936 and 48.638. No marker gained lifetime from that correction. The lead survives each check performed. Full convergence of field resolution, conductor discretization and swept-path collision handling remains unestablished. Collisions are still checked at outer time-step endpoints. Collective plasma effects, self-consistent fields and engineering feasibility are outside this study. The baseline-matched current density is about 2.37 billion A/m², so this is not a buildability certificate.

## What the peers actually built and changed

Four continuing Astra participants worked in two rooms, with no preset planner or critic role. Each team discussed choices, wrote instruments, ran experiments, interpreted failures, and revised candidates. The intended Fable participants both hit a provider session limit with zero reported tokens. They were explicitly replaced with fresh Astra peers in both arms; original Astra sessions and the original absolute wall deadline were preserved. No Fable work is claimed for this run.

Both teams built reusable geometry checks and analysis tools. In the construction arm, a peer argument changed an intended torus-width test into a turn-count test. Its small gain and wall-to-wire loss trade motivated a spacing experiment. Another proposed combination failed clearance, so shared preflight results selected a valid fallback. A paired-lifetime analyzer checked common particle hashes and separated launch-radius cohorts; one observed benefit occurred in a different cohort than predicted. The direct team also changed tests after peer criticism and used a final paired amplitude experiment to choose opposite refinement directions in its two branches.

These are corroborated examples of collaboration changing actual experiments. They are not proof that the instruments caused the advantage. The construction team proposed its fruitful fat-pair family before its instruments succeeded; prior evidence, the search basin and chance could explain part or all of the difference. Nor did this run show an initially slower method later crossing over: the construction arm's first screened design already led. The broad delayed-discovery hypothesis needs independent team repetitions and other goals.

## Work and cost retained in the record

Direct used all 12 screening submissions; construction deliberately stopped after 9. All 21 submitted designs were geometrically valid and passed the specified numerical checks. Geometry tools rejected other proposals before full simulation. Five failed local instrument attempts, duplicate work, negative screens and the model-availability amendment remain recorded.

Each arm completed seven native activations and one failed Fable start. Reported input plus output was 13,098,066 tokens for direct and 10,035,836 for construction, including 22,325,632 cached input tokens across the two arms. An independent audit matched every activation to per-turn counters and their sums to final session totals; there is no resume double-counting. Cached input is already included, and these numbers are not dollar costs. Root/reviewer token use and ad hoc tools are unmetered common work.

Screening evaluator time was 153.39 seconds for direct and 116.13 for construction. Recorded local instrument experiments totaled 119.56 and 79.38 seconds respectively, including failures. These are summed process durations, not elapsed time. Checkpoint usage was sampled about every three seconds and omits still-unreported in-flight token work. Equal ceilings did not produce equal actual usage.

There were **47 actual particle-evaluation calls**: two common baseline setup runs (one superseded), 21 screens, 15 held-out validations, six grid/time-step checks, and three segment-collision diagnostics. Post-lock validation and diagnostics took 834.28 summed evaluator seconds; some independent batches ran concurrently. The first baseline setup was preserved and separately charged, not silently discarded.
